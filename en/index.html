Module II

Key Synaps Components: Your AI Tools

Synaps Chat, Deep Research, Gemini Notebook, and the complete No-Code and Pro-Code Agent architecture.

2.1

What is Synaps Chat and what is its purpose?

Synaps Chat (also called Synaps Search) is the primary conversational interface of Gemini Enterprise, designed to answer queries, generate content, and streamline daily tasks as your smart assistant.

🔄 How does it work and what does it integrate?

You input an instruction or prompt and Gemini coordinates capabilities with Google Search and authorized corporate connectors. It can query in real time your internal repos (Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar) or enterprise tools (Salesforce, CRM). You can type @ to invoke specialist agents directly within the chat.

🧠"Memorize" Function

Saves your persistent preferences (format, tone, or active projects). If a prompt contradicts a saved memory, your current manual instruction takes precedence.

📌Pinned Chats

Used strictly for your daily visual organization. They do not increase context relevance for the model or impact other independent sessions.

⏳ 180-Day Expiration Rule

All chats have a maximum lifespan of 180 days. Synaps Chat is not a file archive; make sure to save your key prompts and final deliverables in Google Drive or your usual work folders.

What it CANNOT do: Run continuous unattended processes (use Workflow Agents for that), connect to unauthorized external databases, or switch to unvetted models (it runs strictly on Gemini Pro and Flash enterprise editions).

2.2

What is Deep Research and when should I use it?

Deep Research is a Synaps autonomous agent that acts as an augmented analyst. It is capable of cross-referencing, comparing, and synthesizing information from multiple web sources and files for complex strategic analysis.

💎 Ideal Use Cases
  • Deep analysis of competitors, tenders, and market benchmarking.
  • Generating trend reports and 360° commercial intelligence.
  • Building business pitches backed by heavy documentation.
⚠️ Guidelines & Limitations
  • High resource consumption: Performs multiple autonomous searches in loops and can take up to 10 minutes. Use carefully.
  • No Paywall Access: Crawls the public web; cannot bypass log-ins or paywalls.
  • Precise Scope: Narrow down your goal in the prompt to prevent generic or diluted reports.
  • Active Verification: It cites web links, but you should always double-check the quoted extracts.

What is Gemini Notebook and how does it help me become a subject expert?

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) lets you build your own **expert thematic companion** grounded in your reference documents (contracts, audits, guidelines, CVs, policies, or manuals). It will answer **ONLY based on the sources you upload**, citing the exact page and paragraph.

Up to 300 Authorized sources per notebook (PDFs, Google Docs, Slides, URLs, YouTube videos, or audio)
500,000 Words per source (up to 200 MB per local file), processing hundreds of pages instantly
Audio Podcast Dynamic two-host audio summaries, study guides, structured FAQs, and conceptual maps
💡 Notebook Best Practices

Create dedicated notebooks for single topics or clients. Remember there is no automatic live synchronization: if a file changes, re-import it and remove outdated sources.

2.5

How do I decide which solution or agent to use? (Decision Tree)

Our rule of thumb is to **always start with the simplest solution** before scaling up:

1. Is it a one-off task?
YES ➔ Use Synaps Chat with a simple Prompt.
2. Are there recurring instructions?
NO ➔ Save a reusable Saved Prompt for you or your team.
3. Does it require complex logic or external APIs?
YES ➔ Scale to a Pro-Code Agent built with our tech team.
4. No-Code automation?
• If it's an interactive conversation ➔ Chat Agent.
• If it's a background step-by-step process (e.g., Gmail to Sheets) ➔ Workflow Agent.

Module III

Mastering Prompt Engineering

The art of instructing Gemini, the standard P.A.C.T.E. methodology, and proven advanced techniques.

3.1

What is prompt engineering and why is it so important for using Synaps?

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing and structuring precise instructions to retrieve the best possible output from Google's Gemini models.

💡 Key Principle: Your prompt's quality directly determines the output's quality. Treat AI as your highly capable but literal assistant, NEVER as a mind-reading magic oracle!
3.2

What is the P.A.C.T.E. method for creating effective prompts?

The P.A.C.T.E. method is Webedia's corporate standard to construct precise and contextualized instructions:

P
Persona
The role or specialty the AI assumes.
E.g.: «Act as a Senior UX Designer specialized in e-commerce».
A
Action
The specific, broken-down task to execute.
E.g.: «Audit the navigation and draft design recommendations».
C
Context
Background, constraints, and target audience.
E.g.: «Targeting a young audience in Spain browsing on mobile».
T
Tone
Expected style or register of communication.
E.g.: «Corporate, constructive, direct, and concise».
E
Element
The exact format or structure of the output.
E.g.: «A table with columns: Issue, Solution, and Impact».
3.3

Are there advanced tips and techniques for high-quality prompts?

In addition to the P.A.C.T.E. structure, apply these formal and proven techniques:

🎯 The 3 Prompting Techniques

⚡ Zero-Shot

«Just tell it what you want»

Requesting the task directly without providing examples. Very fast for simple tasks, but offers less control for complex requests.

🎯 One-Shot

«Guide it with one example»

Including 1 solved example. This helps the AI understand the expected tone, formality, and structure.

💎 Few-Shot

«Show, don't just tell»

Providing 2 to 3 concrete examples of the output. This is highly recommended for tasks with strict guidelines or classification rules.

🪄 Pro Tip: Auto-Optimization (Meta-Prompting)

When working in Synaps Chat and polishing a result message after message, don't manually copy and paste your edits. Once satisfied, ask Gemini:

«Now review our entire conversation and consolidate it into a single master prompt, structured with the P.A.C.T.E. methodology, so I can save it to my library and reuse it in the future with a single click».

Best Practices and Recommended Habits

  • Markdown Structuring: Use headings (#), lists, and bold text (\*\*) to structure your prompts; Gemini organizes logic better this way.
  • Clarifying Prompting: Append at the end: «Before drafting, ask me 3 questions to ensure you fully understand my objective».
  • Multimodal Capture: Drag screenshots, graphs, or tables directly into the chat to analyze both text and image simultaneously.
  • Negative Constraints: Clearly state what NOT to do (e.g., «Avoid overly complex jargon and do not mention competing brands»).
  • Define Length: Specify length constraints (e.g., «in exactly 3 paragraphs of 50 words each»).
  • Order of Constraints (Primacy/Recency): Models pay the most attention to the beginning and end of your instruction; place critical limits there.
  • Self-Correction: Ask the AI to critique its work: «Review your draft and identify any potential ambiguities or factual errors».

4.2

How can I make sure that AI outputs are correct and not "hallucinations"?

A "hallucination" happens when a model confidently invents incorrect facts. To prevent them:

🔍
Validate with Google: Use the doublecheck button in Gemini to search-verify claims in real time.
📌
Verify Citations: Click on footnotes in Gemini Notebook or Synaps to review the source paragraph.
🧠
Human Evaluation: Treat outputs as raw drafts; you carry the responsibility to verify key facts.
🔄
Strict Alignment: Instruct the AI: «If the answer cannot be verified in the sources provided, respond explicitly: "I do not have access to this information in my verified sources"».

You = The Pilot

What other precautions should I keep in mind when using AI?
  • Data Classification: Treat information you upload as internal working documents and avoid entering non-anonymized customer details or highly sensitive corporate metrics without prior approval.

  • Diverse Perspectives: Cross-reference AI-generated summaries with teammate opinions to avoid confirmation biases or narrow analyses.

Best Practices & Technical Support

Essential guide to troubleshooting workspace connectors, optimizing output quality, and structuring your chat workspaces.

7.1

Why isn't my agent finding files or throwing errors? (Connectors)

The single most common cause for an agent returning blank results or throwing a search error is that workspace connectors are turned off or missing permissions.

⚠️ The Importance of Active Connectors

Connectors act as the "eyes and ears" of your agent. If the Google Workspace connectors (Gmail, Drive, Calendar, etc.) are disabled, the system has no technical permission to access your workspace and queries will fail.

7.2

What can I do if the agent doesn't deliver the exact result I need?

Interaction with AI is conversational and iterative. If the initial response falls short, utilize these straightforward adjustments:

  • Switch the Model: In the configuration bar, toggle between the different versions of Gemini (e.g., swapping from fast/efficient models to highly logical, reasoning-focused ones). Complex, multi-step queries often perform much better on reasoning models.
  • Try another Agent: If you are working in a generalized chat, invoke a specialized agent from your directory using the @agent_name command to handle that sub-task.
  • Give Feedback to the Agent: You do not need to restart your chat. Command edits on the active message. E.g., *"This tone is too formal, rewrite it more conversationally"* or *"Omit the budget estimations and focus purely on the calendar schedule"*.
7.3

When and why should I start a completely new chat session?

It is easy to keep a single chat open for days, but this degrades system focus and slows down response times. **We recommend starting a fresh, clean conversation in the following scenarios:**

🧹Topic Shift

If you switch from writing an email template to mapping out cost structures, start a fresh chat. This prevents old parameters from confusing your new objective.

📏Endless Conversations

If a chat becomes extremely long, the agent carries too much background data. It will consume more memory, respond slower, or start "forgetting" initial instructions.

🔄Too Many Refinements

If you have refined or corrected a prompt several times and the output has drifted, start a clean chat and submit your final, corrected prompt.

7.4

What is the best architecture to structure workflow agents?

When building complex multi-step automations and flows, avoid the classic pitfall of cramming all steps into a single, massive agent.

💡 The Modularity Rule: It is infinitely better to build **small, specialized agents for each step of your workflow** than one all-inclusive, massive agent.

Why is a modular architecture superior?

  • Higher Reliability: A specialized agent focuses 100% of its instructions on one precise outcome (e.g., only data extraction or only translation), reducing hallucinations to a minimum.
  • Easier Maintenance: If one step of your workflow changes, you only need to modify that single agent's prompts without risking the rest of the flow.
  • Component Reusability: You can reuse that specific micro-agent in other workflows across your department.
